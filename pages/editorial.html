Editorial standards

How Football Training Lab creates content

Football Training Lab publishes practical educational content for amateur and developing association-football players. Our articles are produced through an AI-assisted editorial workflow with automated quality controls. We do not present the site as a substitute for a qualified coach, clinician, dietitian or other professional.

Who is credited

Articles are credited to the Football Training Lab Editorial Team. This is a site-level editorial byline for our publishing workflow, not a claim that a named individual coach or medical professional personally wrote or reviewed every article.

How topics are selected

Topics come from an editorial queue focused on football training, fitness, strength, recovery, nutrition and equipment. We may also use aggregated Google Search Console data to identify questions that football players are already searching for and to find existing pages that could be improved.

How articles are produced

Drafts are generated with AI assistance and then pass through automated checks before publication. The workflow is designed to keep every article focused on association football, remove duplicate or irrelevant material, improve internal linking and reduce unsupported claims.

For equipment content, named product recommendations are restricted to product information that has been deliberately supplied to the generator. The workflow is instructed not to invent current prices, stock, specifications, personal testing, certifications or endorsements.

Evidence and sourcing policy

Concrete health, nutrition, hydration, supplement, recovery and performance claims receive an additional evidence check. The system is designed to avoid publishing fixed improvement timelines, universal supplement or nutrition doses, or guaranteed percentage effects when those claims are not directly supported.

When a claim matches a deliberately approved source, the article may link to that source. The current approved source pool is deliberately small and includes UEFA football-nutrition guidance, the IOC consensus statement on sports supplements, World Health Organization physical-activity guidance and NHS dehydration guidance. The system is not allowed to invent references or treat an unrelated source as proof for a claim.

If a quantified claim cannot be supported safely, the automated guardrail replaces it with cautious wording that makes clear that individual needs, recovery and training responses vary. This evidence check is applied to newly generated articles and also scans the existing article library.

Accuracy and safety controls

Our publishing rules prohibit invented studies, statistics and professional endorsements. Articles should not claim that an exercise or product prevents injury, and they should not diagnose or treat medical conditions. Health, nutrition and recovery information is intended as general educational guidance.

Automated guardrails also look for unsupported authority language such as claims that advice is expert-approved, scientifically proven or professionally recommended when the article does not contain evidence supporting those statements.

How existing articles are updated

Search performance data may be used to improve one eligible article at a time. These changes are deliberately limited and may include a clearer title, meta description, quick answer or internal links. The system records optimization history and waits before changing the same page again so that performance can be measured rather than repeatedly rewriting content.

Advertising and commercial independence

Football Training Lab may earn revenue from advertising and qualifying purchases. Advertising relationships do not determine the conclusions of our guides, and the editorial workflow is kept separate from ad placement.

Corrections

If you find an error, outdated statement or unclear recommendation, please contact us through the contact page. Useful corrections may be incorporated into future updates.
