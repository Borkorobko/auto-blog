Football Pre Season Training Plan: A Practical Guide for Amateur Players

Preparing for the upcoming football season is essential for amateur and beginner-to-intermediate players who want to improve their performance, reduce the risk of injury, and enjoy the game more fully. A well-structured football pre season training plan helps build fitness, sharpen skills, and get players in the right physical and mental shape ahead of competitive matches.

Related guide: For another practical guide in this area, see our Football Training Plan for Beginners.

Why a Football Pre Season Training Plan Matters

Pre season training isn’t just about running laps or kicking the ball around. It’s an important phase that prepares your body and mind for the demands of football—whether you play in a local amateur league or just enjoy competitive matches with friends. Without proper preparation, you risk poor performance, fatigue, or injury once the season begins.

For amateur players, a thoughtful training plan:

  • Builds cardiovascular and muscular endurance important for 90 minutes of football
  • Improves strength, speed, and agility to be more effective on the pitch
  • Enhances technical skills like dribbling, passing, and shooting
  • Reduces likelihood of injuries such as muscle strains or joint problems
  • Helps establish a mindset and routine useful for consistent progress

Detailed Practical Advice for Your Pre Season Training

To maximize your preparation, your plan should cover 3 main components: physical conditioning, skill work, and recovery. Here’s what to include in each:

  • Physical conditioning: Focus on endurance, strength, speed, and flexibility. Endurance is crucial for maintaining energy throughout matches and involves aerobic activities such as steady-state running or cycling.
  • Strength training: Include exercises to strengthen your lower body (quads, hamstrings, calves), core (abs and lower back), and upper body (shoulders and arms) to improve balance and power. Bodyweight exercises like squats, lunges, push-ups, and planks are a good start.
  • Speed and agility drills: Short sprints, shuttle runs, and ladder drills help simulate football-specific movements, improving quick changes of direction and acceleration.
  • Skill work: Dedicate time to ball control, passing accuracy, shooting, and dribbling drills. Practice both feet to improve versatility.
  • Recovery: Never skip warm-up and cool-down routines. Include dynamic stretches before training and static stretches afterward to improve flexibility and reduce injury risk. Rest days and adequate sleep are also vital.

Sets, Repetitions, and Frequency

As a beginner or intermediate player, aim for 3-5 training sessions per week during the pre season period (usually 6-8 weeks before competition). Structure sessions with:

Training Component Frequency per Week Duration per Session Rest Periods
Endurance (running/cycling) 2-3 20-40 minutes 3-5 minutes active rest
Strength training (bodyweight/weights) 2-3 30-45 minutes 30-60 seconds between sets
Speed and agility drills 2 15-30 minutes 1-2 minutes between sprints
Skill work (ball control, passing) 3-4 20-30 minutes Short breaks as needed

If you experience sharp or persistent pain—especially joint or muscle pain—reduce the intensity or stop and seek professional advice. Some muscle soreness is normal, but pain that affects your movement or worsens is a warning sign.

Step-by-Step 8-Week Football Pre Season Training Routine

  1. Weeks 1-2: Base Fitness and Skill Focus
    Emphasize easy endurance runs for 20 minutes twice per week. Add strength training with bodyweight exercises (e.g., 3 sets of 10 squats, lunges, push-ups, and planks held for 30 seconds). Skill sessions (ball control drills, passing) 3 days per week.
  2. Weeks 3-4: Increasing Intensity and Volume
    Extend endurance runs to 30 minutes, introduce interval training (e.g., 4 x 1-minute faster runs with 2 minutes walking between). Increase strength sets to 4 and repetitions to 12-15. Include speed drills 2 days a week (shuttle runs, ladder drills).
  3. Weeks 5-6: Speed, Agility, and Match Simulations
    Maintain endurance runs or switch one session to cycling for variety. Add sprint intervals (10 x 20 meters). Strength exercises can include light weights if available. Skill work includes shooting drills and small-sided games to simulate match conditions.
  4. Weeks 7-8: Taper and Match Readiness
    Reduce endurance volume for recovery—shorter runs of 15-20 minutes. Emphasize agility and tactical drills. Decrease strength training to maintenance level (2 sessions per week). Focus on rest and nutrition to peak at season start.

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

  • Skipping Warm-Ups and Cool-Downs: Neglecting these can result in muscle strains or cramps. Allocate at least 10 minutes to warm up joints and muscles and 10 minutes post-session for stretching.
  • Overtraining: Training too hard or frequently without rest leads to fatigue and injury risk. Listen to your body and allow at least one full rest day each week.
  • Ignoring Technique: Poor technique in exercises or ball skills can reinforce bad habits and cause injury. Use videos or local coaches to check your form.
  • Neglecting Flexibility: Tight muscles reduce performance and increase injury risk. Incorporate dynamic stretching pre-training and static stretching afterward.
  • Improper Progression: Don’t jump too quickly from light to intense training. Increase loads and intensity gradually over weeks.

Beginner and Intermediate Recommendations

Beginners: Focus on building a base level of fitness and comfort with skills. Prioritize bodyweight strength exercises and straightforward ball drills. Keep sessions shorter—around 45 minutes—and allow extra rest days if needed.

Intermediate Players: Add more volume and variability. Include light weight training, hill sprints, and competitive small-sided games for skill adaptation. You can train up to 5 days a week but heat maps intensity based on feedback from your body.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How soon before the season should I start a pre season training plan?

Start your pre season training about 6 to 8 weeks before competitive matches begin. This allows enough time to build endurance and strength gradually without rushing and risking injury.

2. Can I do pre season training alone if I don’t have a team?

Absolutely. Many of the physical conditioning and skill drills can be done solo with minimal equipment, such as a ball and cones or markers. You can also use apps or videos for guidance and motivation.

3. What should I eat during pre season training?

A balanced diet rich in whole grains, lean proteins, fruits, and vegetables supports training and recovery. Drink plenty of fluids, especially water, and consider small snacks with carbohydrates and protein after workouts to aid muscle repair.

Conclusion

A targeted football pre season training plan is essential for amateur and beginner-to-intermediate players aiming to improve fitness, minimize injury risks, and enjoy better performance during the season. By combining endurance, strength, skill training, and appropriate recovery over several weeks, you’ll set a solid foundation to play your best football safely and confidently. Remember to listen to your body, progress gradually, and maintain consistency for the best results.
