How to Improve Speed in Football

Learn about How to Improve Speed in Football with practical football training advice, common mistakes, useful tips and answers for amateur players.

Updated 2026-08-09Practical player guide

If you're an amateur or beginner-to-intermediate football player aiming to boost your speed on the pitch, this article is designed specifically for you. Improving speed in football is not just about running faster; it’s about gaining quickness, acceleration, and efficiency in your movements while maintaining control and safety. Here, you'll find practical, safe, and effective training methods to enhance your speed progressively.

Key takeaways

  • Develop Sprinting Technique: Speed starts with proper running form. Keep your head relaxed and eyes forward, shoulders low, and arms bent at 90 degrees swinging naturally. Drive your knees forward while pushing off the balls of your feet. Avoid overstriding, which can slow you down and increase injury risk.
  • Incorporate Acceleration Drills: Acceleration is critical in football. Practice short bursts of 5-15 meters focusing on explosive starts. Use drills like the standing 10-yard sprint or hill sprints to build this power.
  • Strength and Power Training: Strengthen your lower body with exercises that target your glutes, hamstrings, quads, and calves. Squats, lunges, deadlifts, and calf raises improve muscle power essential for fast running. Core strength exercises also stabilize your form for efficient speed.
  • Plyometric Exercises: Plyometric training helps develop explosive strength and speed. Incorporate jump squats, box jumps, and bounding drills into your routine to increase your fast-twitch muscle fiber activation.
  • Agility and Change of Direction: Football isn't just straight-line speed. Drills like ladder drills, cone drills, and shuttle runs enhance your ability to change direction quickly without losing momentum.

Why Speed Matters for Football Players

Speed plays a crucial role in football. Whether you’re a forward sprinting to outpace defenders, a midfielder making quick transitions, or a defender chasing back to intercept, being faster can give you a distinct advantage. Speed enhances your ability to create goal-scoring opportunities, recover defensively, and respond rapidly to the dynamic situations of the game. Moreover, speed combined with proper technique can improve your overall performance and confidence on the field.

Step-by-Step Weekly Routine to Improve Speed

Day Focus Details
Monday Acceleration & Sprint Technique Warm up (10 min jog + dynamic stretches). 6 x 10-yard sprints at 90-95% effort with 90 sec rest. Cool down stretch.
Tuesday Lower Body Strength Squats (3 sets x 8 reps), Lunges (3x10 each leg), Deadlifts (3x8), Planks (3x30 sec). Rest 2 min between sets.
Wednesday Agility & Plyometrics Ladder drills (3 rounds), cone shuttle runs (4 x 5-10 m), box jumps (3x8), bounding drills (3x15 m). 2 min rest after plyometrics.
Thursday Recovery & Mobility Light jogging (10-15 min), yoga or static stretching (15 min).
Friday Speed Endurance 5 x 30 m sprints at 80-90% effort, 3 min rest between sprints, finishing with a few shuttle runs.
Saturday Core & Balance Planks (3x45 sec), side planks (3x30 sec each side), single-leg balances (3x30 sec each leg), Russian twists (3x20 reps).
Sunday Rest Complete rest or very light activity like walking.

Note: If you experience sharp or persistent pain, especially in your joints or muscles, reduce intensity or stop training and consult a healthcare professional.

Common Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

  • Neglecting Warm-Up: Skipping warm-ups increases injury risk and reduces performance. Always do a proper warm-up with dynamic stretches.
  • Overtraining: Trying to improve speed too quickly without rest can cause fatigue or injury. Stick to a balanced routine and include recovery.
  • Poor Running Form: Many players favor full-speed without proper technique. Focus on posture, arm movement, and foot placement.
  • Ignoring Strength Training: Speed doesn’t come from running alone. Combine sprint drills with strength and plyometric work.
  • Not Progressing Gradually: Avoid jumping into intense sessions abruptly. Build intensity and volume up over weeks.

Recommendations for Beginners and Intermediate Players

Beginners: Focus on learning proper sprinting mechanics first. Start with low-intensity drills and shorter distances, such as 5-10 meters. Incorporate basic strength exercises like bodyweight squats and lunges before moving on to weighted lifts.

Intermediate players: After mastering basics, increase sprint distances to 15-30 meters and add plyometrics to improve explosive power. Incorporate more structured strength training with moderate weights and higher volumes. Focus on improving your agility and changing directions quickly.

Both groups should maintain flexibility work and listen carefully to their bodies. Prioritize rest and avoid pushing through pain. Speed improvement is a gradual process and consistency matters most.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How often should I train speed to see improvement?

Training speed 2-3 times per week is sufficient for most amateur players. This frequency lets your body adapt without excessive fatigue. Combine sprint drills, strength, and recovery for balanced progress.

2. Can I improve speed without a gym?

Yes. You can do sprint drills, agility work, plyometrics (like jump squats), and bodyweight strength exercises (lunges, squats) at home or on the field. However, gym-based strength training can accelerate gains if accessible.

3. How can I prevent injuries while training speed?

Prioritize warming up thoroughly, focusing on proper technique, and never train through pain. Include flexibility routines, maintain good footwear, and allow rest days. Gradually increase training intensity and volume to avoid overload.

Conclusion

Improving speed in football requires a combination of proper sprint technique, strength, conditioning, and recovery. By following structured drills, strengthening key muscle groups, and avoiding common training mistakes, amateur and beginner-to-intermediate players can make meaningful speed gains safely and effectively. Consistency, patience, and smart training choices are your best tools to become faster and more agile on the football pitch.
